## 2. New story premise

Title: THE FAKE SOS

An astronaut and his little AI penguin companion, Wangdoo, travel to a space station to rescue a missing astronaut.

But the distress call is a trap.

While the protagonist investigates a fake SOS, the missing astronaut steals the rescue spacecraft.

The protagonist and Wangdoo must escape before the ship leaves.

The story must be understandable to a child with no science-fiction background.

Every major incident must relate to this single rescue trap.

No time loops, duplicate astronauts, unexplained technologies, or unrelated monsters.

## 3. Opening — 0–4 seconds

Start with an unmistakably dramatic visual:

An unidentified astronaut with a bright orange shoulder patch activates the protagonist's spacecraft departure controls.

A red warning appears:

"YOUR SHIP IS LEAVING."

Cut to the black-suited protagonist urgently watching the security feed, Wangdoo beside him.

Narration:

"You came to rescue him. Now he's stealing your ship."

Cut to:

"ONE MINUTE EARLIER."

Use only this one clearly labeled flashback.

## 4. Rescue mission — 4–12 seconds

Show a simple rescue briefing.

One missing astronaut.

A recognizable bright orange shoulder patch.

A distress signal coming from an abandoned station.

The protagonist docks with the station.

Wangdoo accompanies him.

No complicated mission history.

The audience must recognize the orange shoulder patch when it reappears.

## 5. Make Wangdoo genuinely lovable — 12–19 seconds

The current Wangdoo model looks too simple and emotionally flat.

Completely improve his face and proportions using the uploaded gray-and-white plush reference.

Requirements:

- Large rounded head with recognizable gray and white facial markings.
- Soft plump belly.
- Small yellow beak and feet.
- Rounded cheeks.
- Soft fabric-like material.
- Expressive face rig.
- Bigger, more readable eyes.
- Cute but controlled emotional expressions.

CRITICAL: His eyes must not remain tiny permanently closed black arcs throughout the entire episode.

Create facial states for:

1. Happy — larger smiling crescent eyes.
2. Curious — attentive open eyes.
3. Surprised — large round eyes with visible highlights.
4. Afraid — wide eyes and worried posture.
5. Relieved — softly smiling eyes.

Preserve the identity of the original plush, rather than turning him into an anime character.

Show a brief, affectionate interaction:

Wangdoo waddles to the astronaut, excitedly shows a small useful object, nearly drops it, and the astronaut catches it.

Wangdoo reacts with relief and excitement.

The astronaut gently pats his head.

The moment must feel emotionally natural, warm, and appealing.

Do not use mechanical floating or stiff sliding.

## 6. Locked room — 19–29 seconds

The astronaut and Wangdoo enter the deserted station.

They hear three knocks behind a locked door.

Wangdoo equips his SCOUT WING.

The wing must physically unfold and look like an attractive, recognizable pair of modular wings, not thin glowing rods.

Show clearly how the Wing attaches to Wangdoo's backpack.

The Wing activates a cyan scanning effect.

The scanner initially detects what appears to be a human distress signal behind the door.

The astronaut approaches to rescue that person.

Wangdoo notices something unusual.

## 7. The first real twist — 29–37 seconds

Wangdoo performs a closer scan.

Reveal that the knocking comes from a small electronic speaker.

The SOS signal is prerecorded.

The wire from the speaker leads to the station's communications equipment.

Wangdoo looks frightened.

Narration:

"There's nobody behind the door. The call for help was fake."

This must be shown physically, not merely stated through a floating text box.

The audience realizes someone deliberately lured the protagonist away from the spacecraft.

## 8. The real trap — 37–46 seconds

The protagonist receives a security alert.

Show a clear, large live camera feed from the rescue spacecraft.

The missing astronaut enters the cockpit.

Show a distinct close-up of the SAME orange shoulder patch from the briefing.

The person activates the ship's departure sequence.

Narration:

"The missing astronaut didn't need rescuing. He needed your ship."

Make the visual evidence unmistakable.

This is the main plot twist.

The audience should understand it immediately.

## 9. Physical danger and Wangdoo's importance — 46–56 seconds

The protagonist and Wangdoo run toward the ship.

Use a genuinely dynamic tracking shot.

The ship's departing engines cause a station security barrier to begin closing.

The astronaut reaches for the emergency door.

Wangdoo slips as the barrier closes.

The astronaut turns back instead of abandoning him.

Show the astronaut physically helping Wangdoo escape the closing barrier.

Use believable coordinated character animation.

No clipping, mesh intersections, teleportation, or floating feet.

Wangdoo is frightened.

The astronaut checks whether he is safe.

This is a crucial emotional beat.

Show the bond through action, not explanatory narration.

The Scout Wing is nearly out of energy.

## 10. Final dilemma — 56–65 seconds

The ship is leaving.

The station is no longer safe.

Wangdoo's backpack contains two compatible emergency Wing modules.

Only one can be powered with the remaining energy.

Show clearly:

A — BOOST WING

Use the remaining power to attempt to catch the departing spacecraft.

Danger: if the attempt fails, the battery will be empty.

B — SHIELD WING

Protect Wangdoo and the astronaut inside the damaged station.

Danger: the spacecraft escapes, leaving them stranded.

Explain the consequences in everyday language.

The decision must be easy to understand.

The astronaut looks toward Wangdoo.

Wangdoo looks up with large, worried eyes.

The decision belongs to the astronaut and viewer.

Hold the options for approximately five to six seconds, with the departing ship visible in the background.
