A discovery.

An unexpected event.

A physical reaction.

A new danger.

## 4. New story — THREE KNOCKS

### Scene 1: 0–3 seconds — Immediate hook

Begin with three loud metallic knocks.

A heavy locked station door visibly shakes.

Wangdoo's scanner says:

"NO ONE DETECTED."

Show the black astronaut recoiling slightly.

Narrator:

"Someone's knocking. But Wangdoo says the room is empty."

Cut to an earlier moment.

No lengthy opening titles.

### Scene 2: 3–10 seconds — The rescue mission

Show the spacecraft approaching the station.

A rescue briefing displays a photograph of one missing astronaut.

IMPORTANT VISUAL CLUE:

The missing astronaut has a distinctive BRIGHT ORANGE SHOULDER PATCH.

Make this patch easily recognizable.

The narrator explains:

"You came here to rescue an astronaut who went missing."

Do not introduce a complicated mission backstory.

Show the protagonist docking.

### Scene 3: 10–16 seconds — Wangdoo

Show Wangdoo inside the cockpit.

He is a soft gray-and-white plush-like penguin with small yellow feet, a rounded gray head, small smiling eyes, and a tiny scanner backpack.

He waddles to the black astronaut.

The astronaut gently checks his equipment.

Narrator:

"This is Wangdoo. Your little AI guide."

Wangdoo displays a route to the missing astronaut's last known location.

Make Wangdoo noticeably more appealing than the current simple model.

Use the uploaded real penguin reference where available.

### Scene 4: 16–25 seconds — Empty station

Show the astronaut and Wangdoo walking through the station.

The station appears recently occupied.

A cup remains on a desk.

An abandoned flashlight rolls slowly across the floor.

A chair is overturned.

The astronaut looks into an empty room.

Narrator:

"But when you arrive, everyone is gone."

The three knocks return.

Wangdoo stops walking.

His scanner activates.

### Scene 5: 25–35 seconds — Escalating danger

Wangdoo scans the locked door.

"NO ONE DETECTED."

Another three knocks.

This time, the metal door visibly flexes.

The astronaut steps backward.

Wangdoo hides behind his leg without intersecting the model.

The corridor lights flicker.

"SCANNER ERROR."

The astronaut activates a flashlight.

The knocking becomes faster.

Do not simply repeat the same scanner graphic.

The physical environment must respond.

### Scene 6: 35–45 seconds — The rescue becomes a trap

A warning appears on the astronaut's wrist device.

"SHIP SECURITY ALERT."

The astronaut opens the live camera from his own spacecraft.

An unidentified person is entering the cockpit.

The figure activates the ship's departure system.

Cut to a close-up of the person's shoulder.

The same ORANGE PATCH from the rescue briefing is visible.

Repeat the briefing photograph very briefly if needed for instant recognition.

Narrator:

"Wait. That's the astronaut you came to save."

A departure countdown begins.

The missing astronaut appears to be stealing the only spacecraft that can take the protagonist home.

Make the visual match unambiguous.

Do not explain the person's motivation.

### Scene 7: 45–54 seconds — Actual action

The protagonist immediately runs toward the spacecraft.

Use a dynamic tracking camera.

The departure alarm intensifies.

Wangdoo waddles as fast as he can but falls behind.

He stumbles near a doorway.

The astronaut stops.

He looks toward the spacecraft.

Then back toward Wangdoo.

He chooses to return and help Wangdoo.

Animate this physical action convincingly.

No geometry intersection.

Show that the astronaut cares about his companion.

The decision costs valuable time.

The countdown continues.

The locked door begins shaking again.

### Scene 8: 54–62 seconds — The dilemma

The protagonist reaches the junction between:

- The corridor back to his spacecraft.
- The locked room where somebody might be trapped.

The manual door release takes time.

The spacecraft departure countdown is nearly complete.

Wangdoo's scanner cannot determine who is behind the door.

Wangdoo presents the two possible paths.

He cannot make the decision.

Narrator:

"You can't save everyone and stop the ship. What do you do?"

Display:

A — OPEN THE DOOR

Someone might need your help.

B — SAVE YOUR SHIP

Don't lose your only way home.

Use large mobile-readable choices.

### Scene 9: 62–68 seconds — Integrated AltWing branding

Keep the dramatic decision scene visible.

Do not cut to a plain black advertising screen.

Show discreet but clear AltWing branding integrated with the decision interface.

Narrator:

"Wangdoo can show you the clues. But the choice is yours."

Display:

"WHAT IF YOUR SENSORS ARE WRONG?"

"Try a real sensor challenge on AltWing."

"altwing.org"

"COMMENT A OR B"

Use the real AltWing brand assets.

The promotional copy must be readable without replacing the story.

The real Lost Sensor Lab involves comparing uncertain altitude measurements. Do not falsely suggest that the fictional door scenario is directly playable.

If there is room for an authentic miniature Lost Sensor Lab visual, use one, but only when it does not obscure the A/B choices.

Do not create a separate seven-second advertising end card.

### Scene 10: 68–72 seconds — Final shocking reveal

Return to the spacecraft's live security camera.

The person with the orange shoulder patch slowly turns toward the camera.

Without speaking, the figure raises one hand.

The figure knocks three times on the cockpit camera housing.

Simultaneously, the locked door in the station produces the SAME THREE KNOCKS.

The two sounds are perfectly synchronized.

The astronaut turns toward the locked door in disbelief.

Cut to black immediately.

End.

No additional advertising screen.

Do not explain the connection between the sounds.

The viewer should wonder why the person in the ship appears connected to the knocking at the station door.

This is the cliffhanger that motivates Episode 2.

## 5. Retention and suspense requirements

The opening three seconds must be immediately interesting.

Avoid extended introductions.

Use action and visual evidence instead of technical exposition.

Create suspense through:

- Quiet anticipation.
- Sudden movement.
- Environmental changes.
- A recognizable recurring sound.
- Contradictory information.
- Real physical consequences.
- A clear countdown.
- Emotional concern for Wangdoo.
- A surprising final revelation.

Do not rely solely on text overlays.

Do not include long static shots where characters stand still and the narrator explains what is happening.

Each important reveal must be visible.

Use simple, everyday English.

## 6. Wangdoo quality

The new Wangdoo must resemble the real plush penguin:

- Rounded gray head.
- White face and belly.
- Small curved smiling eyes.
- Yellow beak.
- Short yellow feet.
- Gray hanging flippers.
- Soft, cuddly fabric-like appearance.

Do not make Wangdoo a generic spherical robot or white egg.

His role is to help the astronaut find clues, not make every decision.

Give him expressive physical reactions:

- Freezing at the knocking.
- Hiding when afraid.
- Running with short steps.
- Stumbling.
- Looking back at the astronaut.
- Showing relief when helped.

Check that Wangdoo and the astronaut do not intersect during the running and rescue sequence.
