## 2. Why this video exists

AltWing V1 is a real platform for students to explore engineering, investigate their interests, try interactive challenges, and build projects.

The website includes:

- Find My Wing: eight decision scenarios.
- Explore Engineering.
- Six aerospace engineering labs.
- Three starter projects.
- My Universe and progress-based rewards.
- Engineering-related college and program information.

Do not make AltWing look like a fictional space-survival video game.

The Last Wing is an original entertainment series inspired by AltWing's philosophy.

Wangdoo represents a guide who helps explorers understand their options and discover their abilities.

The astronaut represents a young explorer learning to make decisions independently.

The video should create this emotional connection:

**Wangdoo helps you explore. You decide your path.**

The immediate marketing goal is to convert interested viewers into visitors who try the actual Lost Sensor Lab or Find My Wing experience.

## 3. Creative direction

Genre: accessible, cinematic, interactive space-survival mystery.

Length: approximately 68 seconds.

Target audience: teenagers and young adults, including people with no previous interest in engineering.

Visual style: polished, atmospheric 3D animated game cinematic.

Tone: exciting, mysterious, slightly frightening, occasionally charming.

Storytelling: simple enough for a ten-year-old to follow.

Avoid complicated science-fiction lore, technical jargon, multiple timelines, unexplained technology, and excessive mission data.

A single episode must contain:

- One instantly understandable opening mystery.
- A clear reason for the protagonist's presence.
- A memorable introduction to Wangdoo.
- Increasing suspense.
- A surprising visual reveal.
- One urgent decision.
- Two choices with different consequences.
- A meaningful connection to a real AltWing experience.

Do not copy existing survival-animation creators' characters, scenes, scripts, or visual designs.

## 8. Final Episode 1 script

### 0:00–0:03 — The hook

Open immediately on a locked metal door inside a dark space station.

Three heavy knocks come from the other side.

Wangdoo's scanner displays:

"NO ONE DETECTED."

The astronaut slowly turns toward the door.

Narrator:

"Someone's knocking. But the scanner says that room is empty."

Cut sharply to the events leading to this moment.

### 0:03–0:10 — Why you're here

Show a small rescue spacecraft approaching the station.

A distress message reads:

"HELP."

The astronaut checks a simple rescue assignment and docks.

Narrator:

"You came here to rescue a missing astronaut. But when you arrived, something was wrong."

Make the rescue purpose visually unambiguous.

### 0:10–0:17 — Meet Wangdoo

Introduce Wangdoo inside the rescue spacecraft.

Use an attractive close-up of his rounded face.

Wangdoo waddles to the astronaut and activates his tiny scanner.

The astronaut gently checks Wangdoo's backpack.

Narrator:

"This is Wangdoo. Your tiny AI guide. He helps you explore, but you make the choices."

Communicate their friendship through natural gestures.

### 0:17–0:27 — The station

The astronaut and Wangdoo explore the station.

Lights remain on.

A cup sits on a desk.

A chair has fallen over.

Personal belongings remain behind.

But no one is there.

Narrator:

"The lights are on. The rooms are warm. But everyone is gone."

The astronaut notices a door at the end of the corridor.

Wangdoo stops walking.

The three knocks return.

### 0:27–0:36 — Something isn't right

The astronaut approaches the door.

Wangdoo scans the room.

"NO ONE DETECTED."

Another knock.

The scanner flickers.

"SCANNER ERROR."

Wangdoo steps backward and looks at the astronaut.

Narrator:

"Wangdoo checks the room. Nobody's there. Then the scanner stops working."

The astronaut reaches toward the manual door control.

The door display says:

"MANUAL RELEASE: 30 SECONDS."

### 0:36–0:46 — The plot twist

A warning sounds from the astronaut's wrist.

He opens a live security camera feed from his rescue spacecraft.

Show the same recognizable cockpit from the opening.

An unidentified person enters the cockpit and activates the departure system.

Narrator:

"Then you check your ship's camera. Someone is inside."

Display:

"DEPARTURE IN 30 SECONDS."

The astronaut looks in disbelief.

The camera briefly shows the figure turning toward the security camera.

Do not reveal its identity.

### 0:46–0:54 — The impossible situation

The astronaut looks toward the route back to the ship.

Then toward the locked door.

The knocking continues.

Wangdoo displays both routes on a simple map.

One leads toward the ship.

One leads through the locked door.

Narrator:

"Your ship is about to leave. And someone might still need your help."

The astronaut cannot investigate both before departure.

Make this spatial and time constraint clear.

### 0:54–1:01 — Audience decision

Show a strong cinematic composition:

Left: locked rescue door.

Right: corridor leading to the ship.

The astronaut and Wangdoo stand between them.

Display:

A — OPEN THE DOOR

Someone might need rescuing.

B — SAVE YOUR SHIP

Don't lose your only way home.

Narrator:

"Do you open the door? Or run back to your ship?"

Then:

"Comment A or B."

Hold the choices while the countdown continues.

Make viewers genuinely hesitate.

Do not resolve the decision.

### 1:01–1:08 — AltWing connection

Transition directly from Wangdoo's failed scanner.

Narrator:

"Wangdoo can guide you. But the choice is yours."

Display the question:

"WHAT IF YOUR SENSORS ARE WRONG?"

Then:

"That's a challenge engineers face, too."

Transition into authentic footage of AltWing's Lost Sensor Lab.

Show a real interaction where different sensor estimates are compared and adjusted.

Narrator:

"Try making those decisions yourself on AltWing."

Final screen:

"ALTWING"

"EXPLORE. EXPERIMENT. BUILD."

"YOUR PATH. YOUR PACE."

"altwing.org"

"LINK IN BIO"

Keep the final visual clean and recognizable.

The story must lead naturally to the real platform.
