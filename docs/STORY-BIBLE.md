# THE LAST WING — master narrative bible

This continuity replaces the coffee/flower adventure in the earlier films.
The old working films and source remain preserved on
`archive/cinematic-before-master-narrative`. The new films are silent: no
spoken narration, dialogue, speech-driven beak movement or quoted character
subtitles. Music, equipment, breathing, impacts and restrained chirps carry
the soundscape. Dates below are fictional mission records, not user history.

## Premise and characters

2189. Junior aerospace engineer and qualified explorer **ARK-07** undertakes
his first independent rescue mission to **HELIX-9**. Seven researchers have
been missing since the station's communications stopped 48 hours ago. His
training is sound; the uncertain situation, rather than incompetence, makes
him seek reassurance. His face stays concealed inside his matte-black suit.

**Wangdoo** is his Personal Exploration Companion, shaped after a childhood
gray-and-white penguin plush. A small backpack contains sealed electronics,
sensor, lamp and a finite auxiliary battery. His cloth exterior is soft; his
cheerful curved eyes, pale beak/feet and hanging gray flippers remain visible.
The crescent is an equipment identification mark, not an AltWing advertisement.
He scans, lights, measures and offers options. He cannot explain evidence he
has not measured, choose the route, or make a repair without physical work.

The new real-plush photograph requested by the brief is not attached at the
time of the initial implementation. A textual reconstruction can establish
the described anatomy and materials; it cannot be claimed to reproduce an
unseen photograph. The earlier robotic PNG is not the definitive new design.
Fictional personal and training archive images are rendered from our fictional
assets and labelled as in-universe records. They are not real images of the
user's childhood or biography.

## Author-only mystery explanation and constraints

HELIX-9's experimental phase relay creates **bounded, energy-limited temporal
echo pockets**: both data and a small number of physical mission traces can
appear out of order. The six-hour offset is measured before it is explained.
The older wreck is a displaced ARK-07 trace from a later mission pass, not a
random second ship with the same paint. The warning is sent by another pass
of this astronaut; his early warning arrives too late because the relay's
offset is uncalibrated. Logs retain both record time and arrival time.

The unknown presences are local units of the damaged containment/rescue
network. The station unit remains aboard HELIX-9; the surface unit remains at
the ground facility. They share a silhouette and a corrupted protocol, not a
magical ability to cross between orbit and the surface. They respond to active
transmission and pressure disturbances under a repeatable rule. They are
dangerous and initially unidentified. Wangdoo is never possessed or evil.
His failure is measurable electromagnetic corruption of an exposed sensor
link; his sealed local safety controller survives.

Seven real researchers sought shielding in the underground maintenance area
after discovering the effect. Their absence is not a hoax. Later records and
passive measurements locate them. Early episodes do not reveal all of this.

The echo reservoir is finite. Nothing grants arbitrary time travel, endless
fuel, a spare power cell or a reset of a bad vote. The final solution isolates
the transmitter, measures the offset passively, schedules a warning outside
the interference window and drains the relay safely. It combines navigation,
power, diagnosis and communication learned on screen. No new magical device
appears in the finale.

### Planned final scene

The mystery has a usable explanation and the researchers are safe. On a
beautiful new horizon, the astronaut prepares a route and turns on his lamp.
Wangdoo looks up and waits. The astronaut reaches down first. They move side
by side, matching the early station composition but with neither leading.
Only music, atmosphere and a small contented chirp.

“He helped you find your wings.”
